Overview

We develop AI tools to monitor how road networks change over time, based on satellite imagery. This supports urban planning and assessing the environmental impact of road networks. Deep learning can extract road networks from satellite imagery at scale, but relies on costly high-resolution imagery, and licensing often restricts model training. Freely available Sentinel satellite imagery is a promising alternative, which covers the globe every few days. However, this data is relatively low resolution, which makes it challenging to extract fine-grained and thin road structures.

InstaRoad explores road network extraction with 10 m Sentinel-1 (radar) and Sentinel-2 (optical) imagery across South Africa. We built a new dataset, ROSA, that spans all nine biomes and urban, peri-urban and rural regions, and used it to explore three complementary approaches: adapting a geospatial foundation model, graph-based road extraction, and super-resolution. The models can be integrated into an end-to-end geospatial web application framework, such as InstaGeo.

Why Sentinel Satellite Imagery?

  • Free. The data freely available to the public. In contrast, a single very-high-resolution mosaic of Darfur can be quoted at $4 million.
  • Frequent. A roughly 5-day revisit makes it possible to track road development over time.
  • Wide coverage. Consistent global coverage, including remote and underpresented areas.
  • Multi-modal. Additional optical, infrared and radar bands can enhance RGB imagery

The Problem: 10m spatial resolution, results in 1-2 pixel or less than a pixel wide roads.

Project aims

  1. Curate a Public Dataset Cloud-free images with road labels were carefully curated to build a public dataset covering diverse regions in South Africa.
  2. Explore Road Extraction Approaches We explore 3 approaches to examine methods utilising geospatial foundational models, graph-based models and super-resolution.
  3. DeployIntegrate the models into web application framework (InstaGeo). Road networks can be extracted end-to-end from a browser.

ROSA: Roads of South Africa

Tiles
1,247 tiles of 512 × 512 px at 10 m
Splits
65 zones: 883 train / 190 validation / 174 test tiles
Bands
23: Sentinel-2 optical, Sentinel-1 SAR, elevation, urban masks and enhanced RGB
Labels
Road and intersection masks, vector centrelines and graph adjacency lists
Stratification
9 biomes with urban, peri-urban, rural regions
Size
11 GB

ROSA pairs cloud-free 2020 Sentinel-1/2 mosaics, composited in Google Earth Engine, with road centrelines from South Africa's Chief Directorate: National Geo-spatial Information (CD:NGI). Urban zones were hand-picked in every biome; peri-urban and rural zones were drawn by stratified random sampling over biome, urbanisation and road presence. The test split was manually checked against high-resolution basemaps.

Demo

Our road-extraction models can run end-to-end on a web application. We utilised the GlobalUrbanMapper GEE data engine as an imagery source, so the app fetches cloud-free Sentinel mosaics. The predicted roads are overlayed on the imagery in a web map. This allows for practical applications such as detecting changes in road networks over time.
