Overview

Road maps underpin disaster response, urban planning and environmental monitoring. Roads act as both barriers and corridors for wildlife and invasive species, and new roads are an early signal of urban expansion. Deep learning can extract road networks from satellite imagery automatically, but almost all of that work relies on expensive high-resolution imagery of cities, leaving rural and peri-urban regions underserved.

InstaRoad asks how far we can get with free 10 m Sentinel-1 (radar) and Sentinel-2 (optical) imagery across South Africa. We built a new dataset, ROSA, that spans all nine biomes and urban, peri-urban and rural regions, and used it to explore three complementary approaches: adapting a geospatial foundation model, extending a state-of-the-art road-graph extractor, and steering pretrained super-resolution with the road-segmentation objective. The models were then integrated into InstaGeo, an end-to-end geospatial machine-learning framework.

Why medium-resolution imagery?

  • Free. Copernicus data is open-access. A single very-high-resolution mosaic of Darfur was once quoted at USD 4 million.
  • Frequent. A ~5-day revisit makes it possible to track road development over time.
  • Wide coverage. Consistent global coverage, including rural areas commercial datasets rarely target.
  • Multi-modal. Optical, infrared and radar bands; radar sees through cloud.

The catch: at 10 m per pixel most roads are only one or two pixels wide.

Project aims

  1. Curate a datasetROSA: a diverse South African road dataset pairing cloud-free Sentinel-1/2 mosaics with government road labels.
  2. Compare three approachesModel-centric (TerraMind), graph-based (SAM-Road) and data-centric (task-driven super-resolution) road extraction.
  3. Evaluate fairlyA shared, manually cleaned test split, pixel- and graph-based metrics, and repeated training seeds.
  4. DeployIntegrate the models into InstaGeo so road networks can be extracted end-to-end from a browser.

ROSA: Roads of South Africa

ROSA pairs cloud-free 2020 Sentinel-1/2 mosaics, composited in Google Earth Engine, with road centrelines from South Africa's Chief Directorate: National Geo-spatial Information (CD:NGI). Urban zones were hand-picked in every biome; peri-urban and rural zones were drawn by stratified random sampling over biome, urbanisation and road presence. The test split was manually checked against high-resolution basemaps.

Tiles
1,247 tiles of 512 × 512 px at 10 m
Splits
65 zones: 883 train / 190 validation / 174 test tiles
Bands
23: Sentinel-2 optical, Sentinel-1 SAR, elevation, urban masks and enhanced RGB
Labels
Road and intersection masks, vector centrelines and graph adjacency lists
Strata
9 biomes (+ azonal vegetation) × urban / peri-urban / rural
Size
11 GB

Live demo: InstaRoad in InstaGeo

Demo screenshot or short screen recording Save as assets/img/demo-screenshot.webp (or .mp4) and replace this box

Our road-extraction models run end-to-end inside InstaGeo, an open-source geospatial machine-learning framework. We added the GlobalUrbanMapper data engine as an imagery source, so the app fetches the same cloud-free Sentinel mosaics the models were trained on, then renders the predicted road centrelines over the imagery in a web map.
